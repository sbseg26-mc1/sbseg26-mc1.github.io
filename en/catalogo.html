Tools of the proposing group

13 items

The components the chapter demonstrates in operation. It is worth noting the anonymization line, which evolved across three generations and whose progression is itself a lesson in security tool design: the first established the hybrid approach, the second fixed the cryptographic weakness and the third redesigned the architecture for scale.

AnonShield

Tool

Scalable pseudonymization executed on your own premises. A five-stage pipeline: streaming ingestion of XML, JSON, CSV, PDF, DOCX and XLSX; entity detection combining a NER model with 36 regular expression patterns; replacement by HMAC-SHA256; persistence of the mapping in SQLite; and output preserving the original schema.

In the short course: it is the tool demonstrated in Part 1 of Practice 1. The detection strategy is selectable on the command line and trades coverage for processing cost; the chapter's dataset uses the standalone one. The speed-up is above 535 times over the previous generation, on the same dataset and the same hardware, running on CPU alone: the gain is architectural, not GPU-dependent.

EC1
Repository

AnonLFI 2.0

Tool

Second generation of the line. It replaces the public hash with HMAC-SHA256 under a secret key set by the operator, extends processing to structured formats with schema preservation, and adds OCR for text embedded in images and PDFs.

In the short course: it explains why the secret key matters. Without it, an adversary reproduces the mapping knowing only the algorithm and the input domain, which makes it trivial to reverse pseudonyms of small spaces such as that of IPv4 addresses.

EC1
Paper

ANON-LFI

Tool

The first generation, which established the hybrid approach: named entity recognition for what has no fixed syntactic pattern, such as names of people and organizations, and regular expressions for artifacts of determined format, such as IPs, e-mails, URLs and CVE identifiers.

In the short course: it is the anonymization module of the GT-LFI ecosystem, with 97.38% recall over 763 real incidents. It introduced controlled re-identification, with an audit trail.

EC1
Repository

AutoClass-LFI

Tool

Intelligence core of the GT-LFI ecosystem, with a Web interface. It offers five capabilities: automatic classification into categories and subcategories; suggestion of containment actions; playbook generation in Markdown and in Ansible; a human-in-the-loop cycle; and lookup of similar incidents.

In the short course: it is the central tool of the hands-on parts of case studies 2 and 3. The intelligence layer is abstracted behind an SDK, which allows switching between models according to the privacy requirement, and the return of human validations is incorporated into the system's knowledge.

SecLINC

Tool

Reference implementation of the prompting strategies evaluated in the chapter. Written in Python, it takes pseudonymized tickets, assembles the prompt according to the chosen scenario (with or without taxonomy, with or without iterative heuristics), calls the APIs of the configured models and exports results as CSV, XLSX or JSON.

In the short course: it supports the four-scenario experiment over 194 incidents that shows the gain of combining a structured taxonomy with iterative heuristics. It was later incorporated into AutoClass-LFI.

FrameworkPE

Tool

An evolution of SecLINC. It generalizes the mechanism to five prompt engineering techniques and adds support for local execution via Ollama.

In the short course: it is the basis of the experiments with open-weight models executed on premises, which quantify the accuracy cost of choosing privacy.

GT-LFI ecosystem

Project

Learn From Incidents, a project of RNP's Hackers do Bem program. It integrates four modules into a single cycle: ANON-LFI pseudonymizes; AutoClass-LFI classifies and recommends; the PoP Module submits the result to review by interns and validation by experienced analysts; and the educational platform turns validated incidents into learning tracks, lessons, quizzes and challenges.

In the short course: it is the operational and pedagogical frame of the whole chapter. It is what turned the operational cycle into a pedagogical one, closing the loop between operations and training.

PoP Module

Tool

Triage and validation environment of the GT-LFI ecosystem. It gives interns an interface where incidents appear with the labels suggested by the AI for critical review, and gives experienced analysts a consolidated view with the human-AI agreement rate, decision history and prioritization.

In the short course: it is where human validation stops being rhetoric and becomes mechanism. It places validation between classification and playbook generation, and not only at the end, precisely because a mistaken classification produces a correctly generated playbook for the wrong category.

Hackers do Bem platform

Platform

AI-based gamification platform for teaching and learning security. It turns already validated incidents into learning tracks, lessons, quizzes and challenges.

In the short course: it is the link that closes the loop between operations and training. In the evaluation with 35 residents, the mean score between pre-test and post-test rose from 67.9% to 91.7%. The incident classification track was the hardest, with the lowest mean score, which speaks directly to the limits observed in automatic models.

Privacy and anonymization

4 items

Established tools that, deliberately, do not solve the chapter's case. Understanding why they do not fit is the argument that justifies the hybrid approach: here the data is not tabular, it is free text, and the immediate problem is not to offer a formal guarantee about a released datum, but to locate the identifiers scattered through the report.

Microsoft Presidio

Library

Data protection and de-identification SDK, with orchestration of personal information recognizers over free text.

In the short course: it is used by AnonShield's presidio strategy, which maximizes recall at the cost of false positives coming from recognizers irrelevant to the security domain. Precision of 71.9% with the same 96.4% recall as the other strategies, which is defensible when over-anonymization is acceptable.

EC1
Repository

ARX

Tool

Anonymization of tabular data with formal guarantees: k-anonymity, l-diversity and t-closeness. It originates in the biomedical domain.

In the short course: it appears as a counterpoint. Tools like this presuppose tabular data and detection already done, and therefore do not attack the operational problem of free-text reports.

Official site

sdcMicro

R package

Statistical disclosure control for microdata, originating in the statistical domain.

In the short course: the same counterpoint as ARX. It is a mature tool for structured tabular data, not for narrative reports mixed with technical artifacts.

CryptoPAn

Technique

Anonymization of IP addresses preserving prefix structure, which keeps subnet-level analysis possible after treatment.

In the short course: it is elegant and solves well what it sets out to do, but it covers a single entity type. It was used by Jesus Filho and colleagues in anonymizing the dataset labeled with the CERT.br taxonomy.

Models and local execution

8 items

The open-weight ecosystem is what materially enables the chapter's privacy commitment: without it, there is no way to classify without the data leaving the organization. The price has been measured, and it is about 60% accuracy with open models from 8B to 20B, against more than 90% with commercial models.

gemini-3.1-flash-lite

Model

Lightweight commercial model, accessed through an API, with a free tier on Google AI Studio that is sufficient for the short course activities.

In the short course: it is the model that produced the results reported in case studies 2 and 3, and the one Practice 2 calls in real time. The exact identifier is recorded on purpose: commercial providers update endpoints without notice, and reproducing the numbers depends on that information.

Ollama

Tool

Packaging and management layer for open-weight models running locally, which substantially lowers the barrier to entry.

In the short course: it is the local provider of Practice 3 and the basis of the experiments with open models that quantify the cost of privacy. It is the path that makes that argument tangible: with it, the data never leaves the participant's machine.

Library

Library and associated repository that consolidated the ecosystem in which pre-trained models are distributed with open weights and can be executed locally.

In the short course: it loads AnonShield's NER model. The Practice 1 notebook degrades gracefully: if the library is unavailable, a simplified lexical recognizer takes over, and the lesson about coverage remains observable.

xlm-roberta-base-ner-hrl

Model

Multilingual named entity recognition model, which recognizes person, organization and location.

In the short course: it is AnonShield's default NER model, used without fine-tuning. Being multilingual matters, because reports from Brazilian academic CSIRTs mix Portuguese and English, sometimes within the same ticket.

SecureModernBERT-NER

Model

Entity recognition model trained on roughly 503 thousand annotated threat intelligence excerpts, able to recognize 22 entity types specific to cybersecurity.

In the short course: it is the domain alternative to the default multilingual model. It addresses the gap noted in the chapter: generic NLP frameworks do not ship recognizers for the technical vocabulary of security.

paraphrase-MiniLM-L6-v2

Model

Sentence embedding model from the Sentence-BERT family, used to measure semantic similarity between passages of text.

In the short course: it supports the semantic comparison between the model's answer and the human label, which is needed when the model answers in free text. It introduces a dependency that has to be declared, because the choice of embedding model changes the numbers.

Foundation-Sec-8B

Model

Model with 8 billion parameters and continued pre-training adapted to the security domain, published by Cisco's Foundation AI.

In the short course: it represents the third route to narrowing the gap between local and commercial models without giving up local execution. Continued training over specialized corpora improves typical SOC tasks, including alert triage and enrichment.

Mid-sized open models

Models

Among 21 models from 1 to 20 billion parameters evaluated, three stood out in the 62% to 70% precision range across both hardware platforms tested: deepseek-r1:14b, phi3:14b and qwen3:4b. Models from 1B to 1.8B stayed below 20%.

In the short course: they delimit the practical hardware recommendation. The determining factors are the number of parameters and GPU capacity, not temperature: whoever intends to run classification locally should invest in video memory rather than spend time calibrating temperature.

SOAR and response

5 items

For those who intend to build their own environment from open source components. The chapter's warning is worth repeating: the cognitive layer presupposes the previous ones, and applying language models to telemetry that is neither normalized nor correlated produces poor results, regardless of model quality.

Shuffle

Platform

Open source SOAR orchestrator, with a visual flow editor and a broad library of connectors.

In the short course: it is the main option cited in the survey of open SOAR platforms and the integration basis of AI4SOAR, used to demonstrate automatic playbook selection.

Analysis engine that accompanies TheHive, running analyzers and response actions over the observables of a case.

Platform

It combines SIEM functions, endpoint detection and active response in a single open source platform.

In the short course: it corresponds to the visibility and behavioral detection layers, the first two of the three maturity levels proposed for building a SOC from open components.

AI4SOAR

Research

Instead of generating a new playbook, it selects from the existing repository the one best suited to the incoming alert, using similarity learning, with integration to Shuffle.

In the short course: it represents the path of retrieving rather than generating. The security advantage tends to be underestimated: the retrieved procedure has already been approved, which eliminates the entire category of risks associated with hallucination. The limitation is coverage, since novel alerts have no counterpart.

Standards, frameworks and taxonomies

11 items

Classifying an incident requires, above all, a vocabulary. The absence of terminological standardization across organizations is repeatedly pointed out as an obstacle to automation: reports describing the same phenomenon receive different labels, which blocks both the aggregation of metrics and the training of supervised models.

NIST SP 800-61r3

Standard

Revision 3 brought a relevant shift of perspective: rather than treating incident response as a self-contained process with its own phases, it presents it as a community profile of NIST CSF 2.0. Preparation and learning stop being steps of a closed cycle and become continuous risk management activities.

In the short course: the reference taxonomy adopted here derives from it, with twelve functional categories, used consistently across case studies 1 and 2.

EC2
Publication

NIST CSF 2.0

Framework

It organizes security management into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover.

In the short course: it is the structure onto which the chapter maps, function by function, where AI can help and what remains under human decision. Govern is the function that decides how much autonomy to delegate to an automated system.

Base
Framework

NIST AI RMF 1.0

Framework

It organizes risk management for AI systems into four functions: Govern, Map, Measure and Manage.

In the short course: it provides the governance principles applicable to a SOC context, which is where the abstract discussion about autonomy turns into a decision per function.

Base
Framework

NIST SP 1270

Publication

It addresses the identification and management of bias in artificial intelligence.

In the short course: it is the reference that emphasizes the human in the loop as a central practice for mitigating AI risks, and it underpins the design of the PoP Module.

Base
Publication

FIRST CSIRT Services Framework 2.1

Framework

It organizes the work of a response team into service areas: security event management, incident management and vulnerability management, among others. It is deliberately tool-agnostic: it describes what the team does, not how.

In the short course: it provides the vocabulary that separates CSIRT from SOC, a distinction often confused. Both consume the same input, textual reports of adverse events, and face the same triage bottleneck.

Base
Framework

CACAO Security Playbooks 2.0

Specification

OASIS standard for representing playbooks in JSON, with an explicit flow structure: sequential, conditional, parallel and decision steps, plus target agents and commands. Its purpose is exchange between organizations and orchestration on SOAR platforms.

In the short course: it is the format required in the output of Practice 3, and what makes automatic validation possible. Syntactic validation checks conformance to the schema; semantic validation checks whether the referenced steps exist, whether the graph is reachable and free of undue cycles, and whether no command uses a tool outside the authorized list.

Specification

MITRE ATT&CK

Knowledge base

It catalogs adversary tactics and techniques observed in real incidents, and is the reference vocabulary for threat intelligence.

In the short course: it serves as context in playbook generation, but the chapter makes an important caveat: ATT&CK describes how an adversary acts, not what kind of incident occurred, and therefore does not replace a classification taxonomy.

ENISA reference taxonomy

Taxonomy

Incident classification taxonomy designed to harmonize communication among European CSIRTs.

In the short course: it is part of the landscape of vocabularies in use, alongside MITRE ATT&CK, the CERT.br taxonomy and the NIST functional categories.

EC2
Publication

CERT.br taxonomy

Taxonomy

Compact set of operational categories adopted in national notifications. In the subset used in the chapter there are six categories: DoS, fraud, intrusion, scanning, web and others.

In the short course: it is used as a contrast to the twelve NIST categories, which allows discussing a point rarely made explicit: the choice of vocabulary affects measured performance as much as the choice of model, and reporting accuracy without declaring the taxonomy makes the number incomparable.

Statistics and categories

Ansible

Tool

Executable format for playbooks, in which each step becomes an idempotent task applicable to the infrastructure.

In the short course: it is one of the three playbook representations used. Idempotence is decisive, because re-running the procedure does not duplicate effects, which matters when the state of the environment is uncertain. The trade-off is that an executable playbook generated by a model is, potentially, an unsafe action vector.

EC3
Official site

LGPD

Legislation

Law no. 13,709 of 14 August 2018, the Brazilian General Data Protection Law.

In the short course: it defines the legal regime of the whole material. The consequence has to be stated without ambiguity: because reversal is possible for whoever holds the key and the mapping, the pseudonymized datum remains personal data. Teams that treat pseudonymization as equivalent to anonymization take on a compliance risk they do not perceive.
